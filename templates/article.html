Mental wellness is the foundation of living a balanced, fulfilling and resilient life. It is not just the absence of mental illness, but a state of wellbeing in which an individual realizes their own abilities, can cope with the normal stresses of life, can work productively and is able to make a contribution to their community. Prioritizing mental wellness means nurturing yourself every day through positive thinking, self-compassion, healthy relationships, and engaging activities that bring you joy and purpose. Breaking the stigma surrounding mental health is crucial. It encourages open conversations, seeking help without fear of judgment and fostering a supportive community where everyone feels safe to share their struggles. Remember, it's okay not to be okay and taking the first step to seek help is a sign of strength.

Why Mental Health Matters

Mental health is as important as physical health. For young people, it plays a key role in forming healthy relationships and preparing for the future. Good mental health allows youth to:

  • Cope with stress and adversity
  • Develop resilience and self-esteem
  • Achieve personal and academic goals

Common Challenges Youth Face

Youth today face various stressors that can impact their mental health.

Depression - Persistent sadness, low energy, or loss of interest in activities.

Loneliness - Feeling isolated, even when surrounded by people.

Stigma - Fear of being judged for talking about mental health struggles.

Wellness Strategies for youth

Proactive steps can help build stronger mental wellness every day:

Prioritize Rest: A healthy sleep routine improves mood.

Eat Well & Stay Active: Nutrition and physical activity boost mental clarity.

Practice Mindfulness: Meditation or journaling can calm the mind and reduce stress.

Stay Connected: Talking with friends, family, or mentors builds support systems.

Seek Help: A professional, teacher, or trusted adult can guide through tough times.

Solutions for Better Youth Mental Health

Creating supportive environments is key. Schools can implement mental health programs and peer support clubs. Parents can foster open communication and model healthy coping strategies. Communities can offer accessible, youth-friendly mental health services. Online platforms can provide valuable information and anonymous support. Policy changes that integrate mental health into primary care can also make a significant difference. It's about building a web of support that surrounds young people at home, school, and in their community, making it easier for them to thrive and seek help when needed.

Breaking the Stigma

Ending the stigma around mental health and wellness is the role of everyone. Mental health is not a weakness - it's part of the human experience. Share your story, listen to others, and advocate for better resources. In vulnerability, we find strength.

Mental Health Resources and Helplines

  • iCALL Psychosocial Helpline: Available via phone, email, and chat app, (Mon-Sat)
  • KIRAN National Helpline: 1800-599-0019
